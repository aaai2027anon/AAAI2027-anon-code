Abstract

StructRecon: Robust Prior-Guided Neural Mapping under Trajectory-Constrained Degraded Geometry

Facility robots require geometrically accurate, navigation-usable online dense maps in large structured facilities with usable structural priors and forward-dominant robot motion. Underground parking facilities are a representative and demanding instance because long aisle-constrained routes provide limited lateral excitation amid repetitive structures, weak texture, and dynamic foregrounds. These conditions make RGB-D observations locally informative but globally under-constrained. Purely visual neural mapping suffers from structural drift, whereas hard external priors can lock reconstructions to biased or incomplete maps.

We present StructRecon, an online prior-guided neural mapping framework with a scalable blocked dual-scale tri-plane backbone, Prior-Guided Importance Sampling (PGIS), and uncertainty-aware bidirectional arbitration (UBA). UBA reduces prior influence under prior-observation mismatch and suppresses corrupted observations under dynamic interference. On an aisle-dominated ICPARK benchmark, StructRecon improves reconstruction accuracy, trajectory consistency, and robustness to prior shifts and dynamic interference. Frozen parameters preserve this ordering on two held-out underground-parking facilities in the same target regime. A complete-route ICPARK-Full deployment further evaluates full-site operation and GTX1660-class edge scalability without removing naturally occurring facility transitions. StructRecon also recovers RGB-D-visible non-prior overhead structures, and an indicative small-N closed-loop robot study suggests that its mapping gains transfer to navigation. We introduce ICPARK, an ultra-large underground-parking dataset with a full-site forward-dominant acquisition trajectory, OSM structural assets, and real multimodal robot observations.

Problem and Core Insight

Local RGB-D evidence is informative; the long-range map is under-constrained

Underground facilities combine forward-dominant motion, weak texture, repeated structure, dynamic foregrounds, and imperfect CAD/OSM priors. Prior-free neural mapping can drift because it lacks sustained cross-track constraints. Hard-prior mapping anchors the route but can lock the reconstruction to shifted or incomplete geometry. Observation-only uncertainty can reject noisy measurements, but it cannot decide when the prior itself should yield.

Prior-free drift

Observation-driven neural, Gaussian, and uncertainty-aware baselines probe whether RGB-D alone can sustain global consistency under weak parallax.

Hard-prior brittleness

Rigid or fixed structural anchoring improves global layout but degrades sharply when the prior is biased or foreground measurements pull the map.

The missing decision

A large residual is not enough: prior-side and observation-side inconsistency look similar in magnitude but require opposite loss updates.

Method

UBA routes the cause of conflict, not only its magnitude

For each ray, UBA uses rendered–observed depth residual eD and rendered depth variance. A confident observation substantially nearer than a confident rendered static surface activates the observation-suppression branch; otherwise unexplained mismatch decays the prior. The branches are mutually exclusive, preventing both anchors from disappearing together.

Branch 1 — observation-side corruption

If Dobs < D̂ − τocc and σ̂D < τconf:

wdata = εdata,   wprior = 1

Truncate a confident near-depth foreground while retaining the static prior anchor.

exclusive
routing

Branch 2 — prior-side inconsistency

Otherwise:

wdata = 1,   wprior = exp(−γprioreD)

Keep the observation active and let a shifted, stale, or incomplete prior yield locally.

Why residual-only weighting is structurally insufficient. Consider two rays with the same residual magnitude. On one, the prior is wrong and the observation is correct, so the prior must be suppressed. On the other, a near-depth foreground is wrong and the prior-supported static surface is correct, so the observation must be suppressed. Any rule that sees only residual magnitude must output identical weights for both rays and therefore cannot perform both opposite actions.

Main paper Figure 1. Overview of StructRecon. UBA is the conflict-resolution mechanism; prior-guided TSDF initialization, blocked dual-scale tri-planes, and PGIS provide the scalable online-mapping backbone.

Prior-initialized TSDF

A 2D Euclidean distance field from CAD/OSM topology is extruded along Z, truncated and normalized to a differentiable pseudo-3D zero-level-set anchor.

Blocked dual-scale tri-planes

8 m × 8 m XY blocks with C=32 coarse/fine features keep the device working set tied to active frustum blocks rather than total mapped extent.

PGIS

Each ray uses a 1:1 mix of uniform and prior-guided samples (τs=1.5), concentrating work near likely surfaces without removing observation-driven correction outside prior support.

Ltotal = λvisual(Lcolor + Ldepthdual) + λsdfLsdf + λodomLodom + λpriorLpriordual

The same data weight also gates all depth-derived SDF supervision (center and free-space terms), preventing a truncated ray from influencing geometry through a side channel; color and Eikonal terms remain unaffected.

Robot Utility

Mapping gains transfer to a shared downstream navigation stack

Four fixed start–goal tasks are repeated four times per method (16 trials). The Ackermann robot uses ZED stereo at 15 Hz, wheel odometry and GTX1660 6GB compute. GlobalPlanner, teb_local_planner, costmap resolution, footprint, margins, velocity limits, task set and timeout rules are fixed.

Shared costmap interface

Near-ground reconstructed geometry supplies traversable/free cells; surfaces above the ground band supply obstacles. Invalid, unobserved and overhead cells are never marked as traversable ground.

Declared localization difference

Neural backends refine RGB-D-to-map pose; Wheel odometry and ParkingTwin use odometry. Robot ATE is therefore backend-level native localization, not map-only accuracy under identical poses.

Offline reference

Timestamp-associated reference trajectories come from manually verified offline LiDAR mapping and SE(2) alignment. Ground uses a shared 0.08 m-grid ROI.

Protocols and Boundaries

Definitions needed to interpret every reported number

Metric, runtime, memory and status definitions
  • ATE: trajectory RMSE in centimeters after alignment to a fixed reference with one archived protocol; no scale correction. Robot ATE uses SE(2).
  • Acc. / Comp. / C.D.: reconstructed→reference mean nearest-neighbor distance; reference→reconstruction mean distance; and their average. F-score uses 5 cm.
  • Runtime: FPS after warm-up, excluding offline preparation. Peak memory is maximum observed mapping GPU memory.
  • Status: OOM = exceeds GPU budget before completion; Diverged = invalid tracking/mapping before completion; Fail = tracking loss, ATE >2 m, or no valid metric; N/A = not applicable; — = unavailable archived metric; Offline only / Not supported retain their literal meanings.
Prior-shift and Real-Dyn protocols

Prior shift. Only the CAD/OSM prior is translated laterally in the map plane. RGB-D, odometry, reference geometry and evaluation frame remain fixed. Each magnitude averages equal left/right offsets; prior-free methods are N/A.

Real-Dyn. Per-frame foreground occupancy is the fraction of valid depth pixels belonging to near-depth dynamic foregrounds in the shared ROI. A frame is dynamic above 5% occupancy. Segment ratios define Low <10%, Medium 10–25%, High >25%: 3/3/4 non-overlapping segments with 1324/1468/1289 frames. After mask/ROI exclusions, 4,081 frames remain.

Visible non-prior overhead protocol

The reference mask includes geometry >0.5 m above navigable ground, horizontally outside a 0.3 m buffer around the 2D prior, supported by at least one valid RGB-D observation, and within valid reference overhead regions. Implementation card
  • Prior TSDF: 2D distance field extruded along Z, truncated/normalized to [−1,1], solid wall interiors filled negative.
  • Blocks: 8 m × 8 m; dual-scale tri-planes; C=32. Geometry decoder: 2 hidden layers ×64, Softplus. Appearance decoder: 3×64 with view direction and normal.
  • PGIS: uniform:prior samples = 1:1; τs=1.5; ε=2×10−3; minimum spacing 0.25 voxels.
  • Base weights: λvisual=1.0 (depth 1.0, color 0.1), λsdf=1.0, λodom=10.0, λprior,max=1.0. Prior weight and γ ramp over 500 warm-up steps.
  • UBA: τocc=0.15 m, τconf=0.05 m, εdata=0.01, γprior=10. Rendering stops at accumulated weight 0.995.

Loss routing and complete objective

For RGB-D ray r, UBA applies wdata to robust rendered-depth residuals and wprior to valid-mask prior-SDF residuals:

Ldepthdual = meanr wdata(r) ρ(|D̂(r)−Dobs(r)|)
Lpriordual = meanr,n wprior(r)m(pn)ρ(φg(pn)−φ̃prior(pn))

The SDF term is Lsdf = wcenterLcenter + wfsLfs + weikonalLeikonal. Center-band and free-space supervision use the same wdata; Eikonal remains unweighted. Color is squared RGB error. The joint objective is:

Ltotal = λvisual(Lcolor + Ldepthdual) + λsdfLsdf + λodomLodom + λpriorLpriordual

Operating boundaries and empirical scope
  • StructRecon requires either structural-prior support or sufficient RGB-D observations.
  • It does not hallucinate unobserved overhead structure and cannot correct severely missing prior structure without observation support.
  • Persistent dynamic occlusion can make arbitration conservative; same-ray prior and foreground corruption retains a prior-trusting residual failure.
  • Device feasibility is bounded by the configured active-block budget. The scaling result is not O(1) memory under arbitrary dense revisiting.
  • Cross-method ranking is on aisle-dominated ICPARK-Benchmark. Held-out sites test frozen-parameter transfer within that regime. ICPARK-Full transitions demonstrate uninterrupted route operation, not balanced region-wise or arbitrary-facility generalization.
